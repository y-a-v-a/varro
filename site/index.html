I

Propositum Why this exists

There is no properly licensed corpus of Latin. There are excellent collections — curated critical editions, epigraphic databases, the digitised Church Fathers — each under its own terms: some free, several NonCommercial, a few silent. Put them together carelessly and the result is something nobody may redistribute and nobody can rebuild.

Claudius assembles one that can be both. Every source is declared in a manifest, every version is pinned, and every licence is carried through to the corpus and on into the weights trained on it. The corpus is the first deliverable, and a contribution in its own right, independent of anything trained on it.

The second is a ladder of small language models trained on that corpus from random initialisation. No English pretraining, no translations, no inherited multilingual representations. Their entire linguistic universe is Latin — and that is enforced, not assumed: English translations shipped inside Latin sources are filtered out, embedded Greek is stripped, and the dialogue used for fine-tuning is mined from the corpus itself rather than translated.

The project is data-bound, not compute-bound. Every model here trains on a laptop. What limits them is how much Latin exists under a licence that can be honoured — which is exactly the question the corpus exists to answer.

¶

Tiberius Claudius, emperor and antiquarian, added three letters to the Latin alphabet — among them Ⅎ, for the consonantal u. They did not outlive his reign.

This corpus goes the other way, and folds every v into u.

II

Via et ratio The approach

Two questions shape everything, and they have different answers: may I train on this? and may I redistribute this?

Duo tramites — two tracks from one pipeline

  1. Viride · green

    Free licence or public domain

    Track A Track B

  2. Sucinum · amber

    NonCommercial, terms known

    Track B only

  3. Incertum · unknown

    Licence unresolved

    Neither track, until settled

  4. Probatio · eval

    Held-out evaluation data

    Never trained on

Track A · publicum

Redistributable

Free-licence sources only. This is the corpus that gets published, and the only one whose weights may be released.

Track B · ad studium

Research only

Adds NonCommercial sources. Never redistributed — its code, results and provenance are published instead. The restriction travels to the weights.

Track membership is derived from a source's licence tier, never set by hand, and it is enforced three times: when a source is fetched, when a corpus is built, and when provenance is audited. A NonCommercial source that reaches a redistributable build is a hard error. The guarantee goes one step further — Track A does not depend on which research-only sources happen to be on disk, because deduplication resolves each track separately.

Tres leges — three invariants

  1. I

    Fetched text is never edited

    Every correction belongs downstream, in code. The moment a downloaded text is hand-patched, the corpus stops being reproducible and the licence trail breaks.

  2. II

    Track A stays clean

    Checked at fetch, at build and at audit. Where a duplicate pair spans a free and a NonCommercial source, the free copy is kept, so text never leaves the published corpus by accident.

  3. III

    Provenance is a standing obligation

    Every build records which sources it drew on, under which licence, in what proportion. The attribution notice is regenerated, never written, and the audit exits non-zero on drift.

Ordo operum — the pipeline, stage by stage

Pars prior · the corpus

  1. IColligerefetch

    Sources declared in a manifest are fetched and pinned — commit SHAs and checksums in a committed lockfile.

  2. IIExcerpereextract

    One reader per format, not per source: TEI, EpiDoc, CoNLL-U, MediaWiki, plain text. A source in a known format needs no code.

  3. IIINotareenrich

    Each work is dated and given a period from an authority file. A date that was inferred says so.

  4. IVEmendarenormalize

    One orthography: u for v, i for j, marks and hyphenation resolved. The sources used opposing conventions.

  5. VDiscernerededupe

    Near-duplicates are marked, never deleted — and only against a text they were directly measured against.

  6. VIComponerebuild

    Corpora assembled per track and period, with validation held out by whole author, not by work.

  7. VIIPartiritokenizer

    A 4,000-entry Latin vocabulary, small enough that inflections are composed rather than stored whole: am · ab · amus.

  8. VIIIMetiribaselines

    A bigram floor for every corpus, so no perplexity is quoted without what the same text allows.

  9. IXAttribuereprovenance

    An attribution notice per corpus, regenerated from what was actually built.

Pars altera · the models

  1. XDoceretrain

    From random initialisation. Recipes are stated in epochs rather than steps, so a corpus can grow into a recipe without rewriting it.

  2. XIProbareevaluate

    Held-out perplexity against the floor, and a memorisation test of generated text against the entire training corpus.

  3. XIIEderebundle

    Only a Track A model is bundled for release, with its exact tokenizer and its own attribution.

Stages fingerprint their inputs in a chain. Adding a source re-extracts only that source, and everything downstream rebuilds once — because the corpus did change.

Decreta — decisions worth not relitigating

  • Measured, not assumed

    Every figure on this page is read from a report the pipeline wrote. Where something is assumed, the manifest says it is an assumption.

  • Licences as offered

    The Latin itself is long out of copyright; the licences cover modern editorial and encoding work. They are honoured as offered, not litigated.

  • Access terms count too

    A publisher that asks not to be mirrored is not mirrored, whatever its licence would permit. The way in is to ask.

  • Silence is not permission

    A source that states no licence enters neither track — not as NonCommercial, not as free. It waits for an answer.

  • Held out by author

    Splitting by work leaked 58% of validation words through authors present in training. Whole authors are held out instead.

  • Recipes in epochs

    LATIN-LII's recipe was written against a smaller corpus that called it undertrained. The corpus grew; the recipe did not change, and fit exactly.

    On the underlying texts

    The Latin texts are ancient and long out of copyright. What the licences above cover is the modern work: the constituted text of a critical edition, conjectures, punctuation and the digital markup. Those claims vary by jurisdiction — the United States rejects “sweat of the brow” after Feist, while Germany and Italy grant a neighbouring right in scientific editions — and this project honours the licences as offered rather than litigating them.

    VII

    Quod restat What remains

    • Ask for Musisque Deoque

      The complete corpus of ancient Latin poetry with apparatus, and no stated licence. The most valuable permission request outstanding.

    • Ask for PHI

      Essentially all of classical Latin, not openly licensed. Writing to ask remains the single highest-leverage action available.

    • Grow the classical corpus

      Around five million words survive every filter. Classical Latin cannot be grown by finding another repository — only by permission.

    • Rebuild on unique ids

      Record ids are now unique by construction. The next rebuild re-verifies every duplicate mark and retrains the vocabulary with it.

    • Serve an interlocutor

      Usable weights behind a simple chat — honestly framed as an interlocutor in Latin, not an assistant.
