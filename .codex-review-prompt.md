## What this is

A pipeline that assembles an openly licensed corpus of Latin, in order to train a small
language model from scratch on Latin alone — no English, no translations, no
multilingual pretraining. The stated constraint is purity: the model's entire linguistic
universe should be Latin. A second constraint is that everything published must be
legally redistributable.

Current state: ~17.25M words from three digital libraries (Perseus, DigilibLT, CSEL),
of which ~4.6M are classical (to AD 200). Six further sources are declared but not yet
ingested. No model has been trained yet.

## What I want, in priority order

1. **Correctness.** Real bugs in `src/`. Weight heavily toward anything that would
   silently corrupt the corpus or produce a wrong number, over anything that would
   crash loudly. Edge cases in text handling are the likely place.

2. **Claim verification.** The docs make many quantitative claims. Pick several and
   check them against what the code actually does and what the data actually contains.
   Flag anything overstated, unsupported, or that does not follow from the method
   described. This is the part I most want an outside eye on, because the same person
   wrote the code and the claims about it.

3. **Methodology.** Are the corpus-construction decisions defensible? Specifically:
   - orthography is normalised by folding v→u and j→i and stripping every combining
     mark, giving a 48-letter alphabet
   - deduplication compares bottom-k sketches of 5-gram hashes, with a separate
     containment rule for part/whole relationships
   - validation is held out as whole works rather than random lines
   - period/date is assigned per work from a hand-curated authority file
   - a 4,000-token byte-level BPE vocabulary was chosen over larger ones
   Where would a classicist or an NLP researcher object? What would they say is lost?

4. **The licensing model.** Track A (free licences, redistributable) versus Track B
   (adds non-commercial sources, research only, never redistributed). Is the reasoning
   sound, and — more importantly — is it actually enforced by the code, or only
   described in prose?

5. **What is missing** that the project has not noticed. Gaps in coverage, method,
   evaluation, or reasoning.
